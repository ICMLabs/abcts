## 3. WHAT IS OPEN — the three ledger defects

All three are **abcts diverging from abcjs in STRICT mode**, which is a defect and not a
divergence. Written up in `ABCJS-DIFFERENCES.md`, gated by `scripts/zzledger.mjs`'s `KNOWN`.
Take one and the gate tells you when it is done.

1. **`Q:3/32=60`** — abcjs draws NO flag *and* colours the whole tempo mark `#ff0000`, its
   own error colour, because its table names `flags.u16nd` which does not exist. Larger than
   the marker predicted: reproducing it is an error path, not a missing glyph.
2. **A `|` bar hint in a `w:` line** — `w:a b|c d` puts abcjs's `c` on the FIRST NOTE OF BAR
   2 and `&nbsp;` on note 3; ours puts `c` on note 3. Every syllable after a hint is on the
   wrong note. Needs barline positions the lyric pass does not have.
3. **A melisma on verse 2** — abcjs prints its literal `_` on every verse; we track melisma
   for verse 1 only. `extraVerses` is a bare `(string|null)[]` and the blocker is three
   layers wide — model, parser, drawing.

**And the ledger is not exhausted**: 97 markers, 16 controls written. The remaining ~80 are
architectural notes and closed self-references, but they were classified by reading, not by
writing a control for each.

## 4. THE METHOD, AND FIVE TIMES IT SAVED THE WORK

**⭐ WRITE THE CONTROL A `ponytail:` SAYS NOBODY WILL WRITE.** 12 of 16 predictions held —
worth recording, since a held prediction stops being a standing risk — and 4 were defects no
gate in this repo could state.

**⭐ AND THE BIGGEST FINDING WAS NOT WHAT ITS MARKER WAS ABOUT.** `parser.ts:365` predicted a
spurious WARNING on `K:Cbmin clef=x`. The prediction was CORRECT — neither engine warns. The
control caught the key SIGNATURE instead: 48 of 168 spellings.

**⚠️ FIVE MUTE INSTRUMENTS, and every one was caught by breaking the thing on purpose:**

- the key harvester's first probe asked `getKeySignature()` after an INLINE change — it
  reports the HEADER key. 231 of 231 "unrecognised".
- its second compared the whole drawn accidental list between two controls — the two HEADS
  already differ. 231 of 231 again.
- the `RenderState` probe computed the leaked value and never EMITTED it, so all three
  variants passed and the comment nearly said the guard was untestable.
- `tests/compat-mode.test.ts`'s measure-width row used a single `^3/2C`, which reports the
  same width in BOTH modes; six shapes were measured before one moved.
- the same file's compat-vs-core row matched `transform="translate(…)"` and found ZERO paths
  in both engines, because abcjs bakes a glyph's position into the first `M`.

**⚠️ AND "MEASURED AND REJECTED" IS ONLY AS GOOD AS THE INPUT THAT MEASURED IT.** Two of the
five quadratics had been measured and rejected IN THE SAME SESSION, on corpus evidence that
was entirely correct and entirely the wrong instrument. See §5.

**⚠️ RE-RUN BEFORE BELIEVING A RED.** Twice a full-suite run reported reds — five once, two
another time — that re-ran green and passed in isolation. It is the load flake `CLAUDE.md`
already names.

## 5. THE QUADRATICS — read this before any performance work

**THE CORPUS CANNOT SEE ONE.** 231 fixtures whose largest is a few hundred notes; an O(n²)
shows up there as a flat, diffuse cost and a profiler over them says nothing. `npm run
scale` measures the SHAPE OF THE CURVE — eight kinds of music at three sizes — and found
five in one sitting:

    warnings.ts  13 copies of a scan from the source's start, PER WARNING   85% of a render
    layout.ts    beamPos filtering ALL anchors for EVERY anchor             49%
    lines.ts     chordEndBeam's lastIndexOf(']') with no floor              39%
    layout.ts    displacementOf's findIndex, inside a nine-pass solve       30%
    lines.ts     lineStart's lastIndexOf('\n') per element        O(n²) on ONE long line
    svg.ts       the `drawn` ordinal scan                         the whole remaining tail

    decorations, 16,384 notes   734 us/note -> ~20      one long line, 8,192   227.7 -> 19.5
    ragtime-nightingale         34.6ms -> 29.4ms        corpus (231 files)     unchanged

⚠️ **TWO OF THE FIVE HAD ALREADY BEEN REJECTED, hours earlier, in this session.** The `drawn`
scan at *"973ms → 975ms on the corpus, 34.6 → 34.5ms on ragtime-nightingale"*, and
`displacementOf` at *"better on the corpus, WORSE on ragtime"*.

⚠️ **AND `displacementOf`'s FIX GENUINELY HAD TO BE LAZY.** The eager map really did make
ragtime slower — a short line's scan is cheaper than the map replacing it. Built on FIRST
USE it is free for short lines and O(1) for long ones, and ragtime is now faster than before
either attempt. **The rejection was right about the fix and wrong about the defect.**

⚠️ **AND THE GATE IS A SCRIPT, NOT A TEST.** It was `tests/scaling.test.ts` for an hour:
inside the 88-file parallel suite a wall-clock measurement measures the MACHINE, and the run
went red on a different file each time and then on this one. A flaky gate is worse than no
gate. ⚠️ Its first shape's first size is often a cold-JIT outlier (2,360 us/note once); the
gate reads GROWTH, so that cannot fail it, but do not read the first cell as a measurement.

## 6. THE THINGS THAT WERE MEASURED AND DECLINED — do not re-open without new evidence

| | measured | why not |
|---|---|---|
| extended's real per-em text metrics | **156 of 691** fixtures | fixes nothing a browser shows — with a DOM both modes ask it |
| threading the 16 switches as a `ctx` | **217 references** | readability, and no safety the save/restore lacks |
| splitting `layout.ts` at its named seams | **75% transitively coupled** to those switches | the seams ARE the 75% |
| `deepFreeze` via `for…in` | 0.1% / 5.9% / 0.3% | under the noise floor |
| `roundNumber` integer fast path | **1.9% SLOWER** | the guard costs more than the round trip saves |
| render-scoped text-width cache | **3.3% SLOWER** | the string key costs more than the character walk |
| `goldenTextWidth` without `split('\n')` | −0.3% then +7.2% | unmeasurable |

⚠️ **The first measurement of "how coupled is `layout.ts`" said 95%** because it matched the
switch names inside DOC PROSE — `ENGRAVE`, a 780-line constant table, came out "coupled"
because a comment mentions `STRICT_TEXT_METRICS`. **A dependency analysis that reads comments
measures the writing.** 95% → 75% on stripping them, and that difference is the whole
feasibility question.
