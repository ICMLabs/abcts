## 🔬 WHAT LANDED, FOR THE SHAPE OF IT

Thirteen, newest last within each family.

**Granularity — a field takes effect WHERE IT IS WRITTEN.** A mid-line `[K:C bass-8]`
pushes its transpose at the clef (`abc_midi_sequencer.js:300-308`), so `CD[K:C bass-8]EF|`
sounds 60, 62, 52, 53 and we shifted all four. An inline `[Q:]` is stamped with the voice's
`durationCounter` at the moment it is pushed (`:355-361`), so `CD[Q:1/4=90]EF|` leaves C
and D at the opening tempo; `collectTempoChanges` keyed the change on the MEASURE and
doubled all four.

**Structure the parser decides, not the flattener.** A slur mark around a CHORD hangs on
the ELEMENT — one pitch or three, `([C]D)` included — while the flattener reads
`note.startSlur` off the PITCH (`abc_midi_flattener.js:579-582`), so it never reaches
`slurCount`; a seven-rung ladder pinned every edge. A tie's `endTie` is stamped on the
element FOLLOWING the tied one whatever its pitch, so an open tie never survives one
element: `C-z C|` is two notes there and was one here, while `C-|C|` and `C-y C|` are
untouched — a REST is `el_type: 'note'` and a `y` SPACER is not.

**Substitutions the SEQUENCER makes that the parse tree denies.** `noteElem.duration =
(elem.duration === 0) ? 0.25 : elem.duration` (`:252`) — abcjs's own tree still says 0, so
reading it would have denied the rule. And `"".split(" ")` is `[""]`, a ONE-element array,
which is why `%%MIDI drumon` with no pattern gives abcjs a drum track holding its `program`
row and nothing else: we wrote three tracks where the file has four.

**Scope — a voice is not a measure.** `tripletMultiplier` is per VOICE over a flat element
stream (`:253-277`), so `(3CD|EFGA|` is a triplet whose third member is the E of the next
bar; both look-ups in `writtenTimeline` were scoped to `measure.events`. A multi-measure
rest is as long as the METER says — `Z3` is `1.5` under `M:2/4` and `3` under `4/4`, asked
of abcjs at three meters, and 4/4 is the one where our whole-note assumption agreed.

**The repeat resolver, three findings, all instrumented.** `addBar` pushes `endRepeat`,
`startEnding`, `startRepeat` in THAT ORDER on one bar element (`synth/repeats.js:16-21`) —
the note at the head of our `resolveRepeats` said so and the code had the last two
reversed. `[]` is TRUTHY, so a quoted `["first"]` that parses to no numbers still pushes a
section whose empty `endings` makes the repeat the PLAIN kind. The gap span's guard is
`section.index - 1` and its END is `section.index` — invisible on a barline-indexed
section, and on the trailing synthetic one it is the first note of the measure. And a volta
on the FIRST measure is a section the bar loop structurally cannot see, because it reads
each barline's volta off the measure AFTER it.
